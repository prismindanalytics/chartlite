ChartLite is an Android app. A clinician records a visit by voice or photo and walks away with a structured clinical note, a billing claim, and a real-time safety check on every drug and diagnosis — running entirely on the phone, no internet.

1. The problem

Frontline clinical workers spend a third of their day on paperwork. The places that need clinical AI most have the least connectivity, and most “clinical AI” tools assume a cloud call per visit — which fails on rural 2G.

ChartLite runs the entire visit on a mid-range Android phone with the data plan off: voice transcription, structured note, safety check against a real medical knowledge graph, encrypted SMS relay.

2. We benchmarked before we shipped

A 3% hallucination on dosages is a coroner’s inquest, not a rounding error. So we built the benchmark first.

12 models — 6 cloud (Opus / Sonnet / Haiku 4.x, GPT-5.5 / 5.4 / 4.1) and 6 on-device (Qwen 3.5 0.8B / 2B / 9B, Gemma 4 e2b / e4b, MedGemma 1.5) — across six clinical evals: 100 synthetic visits; 156 real clinician-annotated Eka Care transcripts; ACI-Bench dialogue-to-SOAP (n=207×5); CRESCENDDI drug-drug interactions; Eka’s NFI Pharmacology MCQA (925 questions); Eka’s Medical Calculator Eval (1,066 vignettes, 26 specialties). Per-(model, case) JSON, scoring code, and methodology open at benchmark.chartlite.health.

Two findings shaped what we shipped:

Gemma 4 e4b is the first on-device model good enough to ship for clinical note generation. ACI-Bench: 82.74, tied with Claude Haiku 4.5 and 6 points behind the GPT-5.5 leader. Real Eka transcripts: 82.6, 5 points behind Haiku 4.5. Zero API cost, on the phone. Where it lags — pharmacology MCQ and drug-dose math — ChartLite calls BODHI instead of asking the model.

BODHI — Eka Care’s medical knowledge graph (779 diagnoses, 1,186 drugs, 812 lab tests, 10,352 symptom-to-diagnosis links) — matters most for the small on-device models frontline clinics actually run. Wiring BODHI into every model lifts raw safety-alert detection by 26–63 points. Honest audit: ~40% is real catch, ~60% is scoring artifact (the model said the same thing in different words; our text matcher didn’t credit it), and ~20% of BODHI alerts are false positives (fuzzy match firing the wrong rule). Net of artifact and noise, value lands where it matters: Qwen 3.5 0.8B 3% → 66%, Gemma 4 e4b 30% → 57%. Top cloud models already near the ceiling gain least. BODHI is a deterministic safety net under clinician judgement, not a substitute.

3. How ChartLite works

  • Voice → text: Parakeet TDT v3 (1.69% English WER) on 4 GB+ phones, Omnilingual ASR (1,600+ languages) elsewhere.
  • Note generation: Gemma 4 e2b / e4b on-device, or Sonnet over the network — same downstream code.
  • Encrypted DB: HL7 FHIR + AES-256-GCM.
  • Safety: drug-allergy, drug-drug, dose-by-weight, vitals out of range, plus BODHI for diagnosis-level checks.
  • SMS relay: a full visit in one 160-character SMS.
  • Billing: ICD-10 → CPT / HCPCS. Sync: phone-to-phone over BT or local WiFi.

4. Multimodal capture: 8 artifacts, one button

Clinicians work with paper. One “📷 Capture clinical artifact” button sends a photo to Gemma 4’s vision and pipes the result into the BODHI safety check:

ArtifactWhat gets extracted
Pill bottle / medication packageDrug, dose, route, frequency, expiry, manufacturer
Lab reportTest, value, unit, reference range, flag
Rapid diagnostic test (malaria / HIV / COVID)Test type, result, visible bands
Vital-signs device (BP cuff, pulse oximeter)Vital, value, unit
Referral letterSending facility, diagnosis, reason, urgency
Vaccine card / Yellow CardVaccine, date, dose number, batch, route
Handwritten prescriptionDrug, dose, route, frequency, duration
Discharge summaryDiagnosis, meds, follow-up, alerts

Tap → photo → Gemma 4 reads the image → Gemma 4 picks which safety checks to run (drug-drug? drug-allergy? wrong drug for condition? emergency triage?) → the result renders with structured fields, the tool calls Gemma chose, and any alerts.

This is how a 4-billion-parameter model becomes safe in a clinical setting: multimodal perception, structured tool calls, deterministic medical lookups.

5. Deployment readiness

Privacy. Voice and model run on the phone. Nothing leaves the device unless the clinician syncs. SMS relay uses AES-256-GCM.

Footprint. Gemma 4 e2b in 4-bit quantization is ~1.6 GB, runs on 2 GB-RAM phones. e4b is ~3.1 GB, needs 4 GB. The app ships both and picks one by RAM at install. The submission demo runs e2b on a Galaxy Fold 7: vision + safety-tool trace takes 30–50 s end-to-end. The video’s wall-clock is honest, no speed-ramps.

Governance. BODHI © Eka Care, CC BY-NC 4.0 — commercial deployment needs an Eka license. Everything else (benchmark, methodology, app) is Apache 2.0. ChartLite is production-ready and awaiting pilot.

6. What we built for this hackathon

In eight days:

  • Gemma 4 vision wired into the model dispatcher; one session handles text and image.
  • Three new artifact prompts (vaccine card, handwritten Rx, discharge summary) on top of five existing.
  • Function-calling refactor so Gemma 4 picks which of four safety checks to run per artifact.
  • Universal capture button on the encounter screen.
  • Multimodal benchmark: ~105 images across 8 artifact types, per-type accuracy + per-field precision/recall on a new dashboard tab.
  • Default on-device model flipped to Gemma 4 based on the benchmark.

Every dashboard number links to its raw JSON. The Methodology tab declares the synthetic-data and self-bias soft spots and records exact API model IDs. If a judge wants to disprove a single number, the raw data is there.
