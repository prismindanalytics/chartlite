The full submission writeup

Why we built ChartLite the way we did, what the benchmark says, and how a judge can disprove any number on the dashboard.

Repo: github.com/prismindanalytics/chartlite (Apache 2.0) Independent benchmark: benchmark.chartlite.health APK: github.com/prismindanalytics/chartlite/releases/tag/v1.0.0-hackathon Live demo: chartlite.health/hackathon Demo: 3-minute video below

1. The problem

Frontline clinical workers in sub-Saharan Africa, South Asia, and rural everywhere spend a third of their day on paperwork. The places that need clinical AI most have the least connectivity — and most "clinical AI" tools assume a cloud API call per encounter, which does not work in a Kenyan rural health post on 2G.

ChartLite is an Android app that runs the entire clinical encounter loop on a mid-tier phone with no internet: voice transcription, structured extraction, clinical-decision support against a real medical knowledge graph, encrypted SMS relay when paper-thin connectivity returns. Nine months of building. The Gemma 4 hackathon is the first moment a small open model is good enough to be the brain.

2. Why most clinical-AI submissions hand-wave model choice

Pick any clinical-AI demo on the internet and you will see "runs locally on Gemma / Qwen / Llama." Almost none of them say whether the model is actually accurate enough to be safe. A 3% hallucination rate on medication dosages is not a rounding error, it is a coroner's inquest. Before we picked Gemma 4 as ChartLite's on-device model, we built a benchmark.

3. The benchmark headlines

We ran 12 models — 6 cloud (Claude Opus/Sonnet/Haiku 4.x, GPT-5.5/5.4/4.1) and 6 on-device (Qwen 3.5 in 0.8B/2B/9B, Gemma 4 e2b/e4b, MedGemma 1.5) — across 6 independent benchmarks: synthetic clinical encounters, real Eka Care transcripts (n=156), ACI-Bench dialogue→SOAP (n=207×5 splits), CRESCENDDI drug-drug interactions, the brand-new Eka NFI Pharmacology MCQA (n=925), and the Eka Medical Calculator Eval (n=1,066). The full dashboard, methodology, model versions, and reproduction scripts are open at benchmark.chartlite.health (Apache 2.0).

Four findings shaped what we built:

  1. Gemma 4 e4b is the first on-device model good enough to ship for clinical SOAP generation. On ACI-Bench dialogue→SOAP (n=207×5 splits, peer-reviewed) it scores 82.74, essentially tying Claude Haiku 4.5 (82.72) and within 6 points of the GPT-5.5 leader (88.48). On Eka Care's 156 real clinician-annotated transcripts it lands within 5 points of Haiku 4.5 (82.6 vs 87.0). Zero API cost, zero cloud calls, runs entirely on-device. Honest where it lags: pharmacology MCQA (63.5% vs Haiku 78.9%) and calculator vignettes (50.2% vs GPT-5.5 68.8%) — but those go through BODHI tool calls, not model memory.

  2. MedGemma is not what you think it is. Despite the name, MedGemma 1.5 underperforms generalist Gemma 4 e4b on every clinical benchmark we ran: −34pp on ACI-Bench, −20pp on Eka Calculator, −17pp on NFI Pharmacology, and a 60% silent-failure rate on simple drug-pair extraction. Same parameter class, opposite results. The medical fine-tune appears to have eroded reasoning more than it improved recall. Eka Care's team independently reached the same conclusion on their internal Indian clinical data.

  3. The smallest Qwen models silently fail on long prompts. Without "think": false Qwen 3.5 0.8B and 2B route long-form output into a hidden reasoning channel and return blank notes. We documented and patched this; teams quoting Qwen-small benchmarks without the fix are publishing noise.

  4. BODHI matters most where it should: small on-device models in under-resourced settings. Wiring Eka Care's clinical knowledge graph (CC BY-NC 4.0) into every model lifts gross safety detection by 26–63 pp. We audited the lift honestly on GPT-5.5's missed dangers: ~40% genuine clinical catch (e.g. synthesizing CURB-65 → EMERGENCY), ~60% substring-match artifact the scorer didn't credit. ~20% of BODHI alerts are false positives (drug-actually-indicated, referral fuzzy-match). Net of artifact and noise, the lift is most pronounced where deployment is real — Qwen 0.8B 3% → 66%, Gemma 4 e4b 30% → 57% — and least pronounced on saturated frontier (Sonnet 4.6). BODHI is a deterministic safety net under clinician judgement, not a substitute. Full audit (with the slide-deck decomposition) available on request.

These findings are not anecdote — they are 7,500 lines of reproducible benchmark code with per-(model, case) cached JSON outputs, public on the dashboard.

4. ChartLite architecture

Eight modules, all on-device first:

  • Voice → text: NVIDIA Parakeet TDT v3 (1.69% WER on English, on-device via Sherpa-ONNX) for English; Meta Omnilingual ASR (1,600+ languages) for non-English; Moonshine Tiny fallback for ≤ 3 GB devices. Hardware-aware tier selection by RAM.
  • Structured extraction: unified JSON schema produced by Gemma 4 e4b on-device or Claude Sonnet in cloud mode. Identical downstream code path.
  • Local DB: Room + SQLCipher, FHIR-native schema.
  • Clinical decision support: static rules (drug-allergy, drug-drug, dosage-by-weight, vital alerts) plus the BODHI knowledge graph (779 SNOMED conditions, 1,186 drugs, 812 labs, 10,352 symptom→condition edges; CC BY-NC 4.0, Eka Care).
  • Encrypted SMS: AES-256-GCM, 92-byte binary encoding so a full encounter fits in a single SMS for ultra-low-bandwidth relay.
  • Patient ID: offline 8-character base28 (KFMT-4WRN format), collision rate negligible at sub-million scale.
  • Billing engine: ICD-10 → CPT/HCPCS mapping, E/M complexity scoring, SAMA tariffs (configurable per country).
  • Sync: BT/WiFi peer-to-peer, three-tier connectivity model (offline / mesh / cloud).

Country configurations exist for South Africa, Ethiopia, Malawi today, with regulatory data drafted for Kenya, Nigeria, US, UK, India.

5. Multimodal capture: 8 clinical artifacts, one button

The hackathon rubric prizes Gemma 4's unique multimodal + function-calling features. Frontline clinicians work with paper. So we built a single "📷 Capture clinical artifact" button that uses Gemma 4 e4b's vision to read any of eight artifact types and pipe the result into BODHI safety checks:

Artifact What gets extracted
Pill bottle / medication package Drug, dose, route, frequency, expiry, manufacturer
Lab report Test name, value, unit, reference range, flag
RDT cassette (malaria/HIV/COVID) Test type, result, visible bands
Vital device readout (BP cuff, pulse-ox) Vital name, value, unit
Referral letter From facility, diagnosis, reason, urgency
Vaccine card / Yellow Card Vaccine, date, dose number, batch, route
Handwritten prescription Drug, dose, route, frequency, duration, sig
Discharge summary Discharge dx, meds, follow-up, alerts

Tap the button → CameraX takes a photo → Gemma 4 e4b vision extracts a unified JSON schema → Gemma 4 then decides which BODHI safety checks to run as function calls (check_drug_condition, check_drug_drug_interactions, check_drug_allergy, check_triage_urgency) → results render as a card with the structured fields, the function-call trace, and any safety alerts.

The judge sees Gemma 4 actively choosing which tools to call. That is how a 4B model becomes safe in a clinical setting: multimodal perception, structured tool calls, deterministic medical lookups.

6. Real-world deployment readiness

Privacy: on-device ASR + on-device LLM means the patient's voice and the extracted record never leave the phone unless the clinician explicitly syncs. SMS relay uses AES-256-GCM with PBKDF2; the binary encoding fits a full encounter in one 160-character SMS so it works on 2G.

Footprint: Gemma 4 e2b (Q4) is ~1.6GB on disk and runs on devices with 2GB RAM. Gemma 4 e4b (Q4) is ~3.1GB and runs on 4GB-RAM mid-tier phones. We tested on a Galaxy Fold 7 (12 GB RAM, Gemma 4 E4B) — vision extraction completes in ~6 seconds per artifact.

Governance: full clinical-decision support uses BODHI (Eka Care, CC BY-NC 4.0; non-commercial unrestricted, commercial deployment requires Eka license). Our benchmark and methodology are independent (Apache 2.0). We have not deployed ChartLite in any production clinic yet — it is production-ready and awaiting clinical pilot.

7. What we built specifically for this hackathon

In the eight days between announcement and submission:

  • Gemma 4 vision integration via MediaPipe's LlmInferenceSession.addImage(MPImage), wired through our existing model dispatcher.
  • Three new artifact prompts (vaccine card, handwritten prescription, discharge summary) on top of five we already had.
  • Function-calling refactor: four BODHI safety checks rewired as Gemma 4 native function calls; the model decides which to invoke per artifact.
  • Universal capture button on the encounter screen.
  • Multimodal benchmark: ~105 images across 8 artifact types from WHO templates and public sources. Per-type classification accuracy and per-field P/R added as a new dashboard tab.
  • Default tier flip: ChartLite's recommended on-device model is now Gemma 4 e4b (was Qwen 3.5), based on the benchmark evidence above.

8. How to verify everything

Independently reproducible:

  • Benchmarks: git clone github.com/prismindanalytics/clinical-edge-bench, pip install -r scripts/requirements.txt, bash scripts/fetch_bodhi.sh, python3 scripts/benchmark_pharmacology_mcqa.py --models gemma4-e4b --limit 50. All raw per-(model, case) JSON is preserved.
  • App: git clone github.com/prismindanalytics/chartlite, ./gradlew assembleDebug produces an APK for Android 8+. First launch downloads Gemma 4 e4b from HuggingFace.
  • Dashboard: benchmark.chartlite.health — every number above links to its raw JSON.
  • Methodology + model versions: the Methodology and Model-Versions tabs on the dashboard declare the synthetic-GT and self-bias soft spots openly and record the exact API handles + Ollama tags used.

If a judge wants to disprove a single number, they have the raw data to do it. That's the bar we want for clinical AI generally.
