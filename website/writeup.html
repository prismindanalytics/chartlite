What ChartLite is: an Android app that lets a clinician record a patient visit by voice or photo and walk away with a complete medical note, a billing claim, and a real-time safety check on every drug and diagnosis — running entirely on the phone, no internet required.

1. The problem

Frontline clinical workers in sub-Saharan Africa, South Asia, and rural everywhere spend a third of their day on paperwork. The places that need clinical AI most have the least connectivity — and most "clinical AI" tools assume a cloud API call per visit, which does not work in a Kenyan rural health post on 2G.

ChartLite runs the entire visit — voice transcription, structured note, safety check against a real medical knowledge graph, encrypted SMS relay — on a mid-range Android phone with the data plan off.

2. Why most clinical-AI submissions hand-wave model choice

Most clinical-AI demos say "runs locally on Gemma / Qwen / Llama" without verifying accuracy. A 3% hallucination rate on dosages is a coroner's inquest, not a rounding error. So we built a benchmark first.

3. The benchmark headlines

We ran 12 language models — 6 cloud (Claude Opus/Sonnet/Haiku 4.x, GPT-5.5/5.4/4.1) and 6 on-device (Qwen 3.5 0.8B/2B/9B, Gemma 4 e2b/e4b, MedGemma 1.5) — across 6 clinical benchmarks: 100 synthetic visits; 156 real clinician-annotated transcripts from Eka Care; ACI-Bench (peer-reviewed dialogue-to-SOAP-note generation — SOAP = Subjective / Objective / Assessment / Plan, the structure clinicians write after every visit; n=207 × 5 splits); CRESCENDDI (public dataset of dangerous drug-drug interactions); the new Eka NFI Pharmacology MCQA (925-question pharmacology exam, multiple-choice, from India's National Formulary); and the Eka Medical Calculator Eval (1,066 clinical-math vignettes across 26 specialties). Full dashboard, methodology, and reproduction scripts open at benchmark.chartlite.health (Apache 2.0).

Four findings shaped what we built:

  1. Gemma 4 e4b is the first on-device model good enough to ship for clinical note generation. On ACI-Bench it scores 82.74, tying Claude Haiku 4.5 (82.72) and within 6 points of the GPT-5.5 leader (88.48). On Eka's 156 real transcripts it lands within 5 points of Haiku 4.5 (82.6 vs 87.0). Zero API cost, runs entirely on the phone. Where it lags — pharmacology multiple-choice and drug-dose math — ChartLite calls a deterministic medical lookup (BODHI, below) instead of relying on the model, so user-facing accuracy is much higher than the raw model score.

  2. MedGemma is not what you think it is. Despite the name, MedGemma 1.5 underperforms generalist Gemma 4 e4b on every clinical benchmark we ran: −34pp on ACI-Bench, −20pp on Eka Calculator, −17pp on NFI Pharmacology, and a 60% silent-failure rate on simple drug-pair extraction. Same parameter class, opposite results. The medical fine-tune appears to have eroded reasoning more than it improved recall.

  3. The smallest Qwen models silently fail on long prompts. Without "think": false Qwen 3.5 0.8B and 2B route long-form output into a hidden reasoning channel and return blank notes. We documented and patched this; teams quoting Qwen-small benchmarks without the fix are publishing noise.

  4. BODHI — Eka Care's medical knowledge graph (779 diagnoses, 1,186 drugs, 812 lab tests, 10,352 symptom-to-diagnosis links) — matters most for the small on-device models frontline clinics actually run. Wiring BODHI into every model lifts raw safety-alert detection by 26–63 points across the 12 models. Honest audit: ~40% is real catch the model would otherwise have missed; ~60% is scoring artifact (model said the same thing in different words, our text-match scorer didn't credit it); ~20% of BODHI alerts are false positives (drug flagged as unindicated when it actually was; fuzzy match firing the wrong rule). Net of artifact and noise, value lands where it matters: Qwen 3.5 0.8B 3% → 66%, Gemma 4 e4b 30% → 57%, smallest benefit on top cloud models already near the ceiling (Sonnet 4.6). BODHI is a deterministic safety net under clinician judgement, not a substitute.

Findings trace to 7,500 lines of benchmark code with per-(model, case) JSON outputs on the dashboard.

4. How ChartLite works

Eight on-device modules:

  • Voice → text: auto-picks the best speech model for the phone and language — NVIDIA Parakeet TDT v3 (1.69% English word-error rate) on 4GB+ phones, Meta Omnilingual ASR (1,600+ languages) elsewhere.
  • Note generation: Gemma 4 e4b on-device, or Claude Sonnet over the network — same downstream code.
  • Encrypted database on the international medical-records standard (HL7 FHIR), with the encryption online banking uses.
  • Safety check: rules for drug allergies, drug-drug interactions, dose-by-weight, abnormal vitals — plus BODHI for diagnosis-level checks.
  • Encrypted SMS relay: a whole visit packed into one 160-character SMS for clinics on 2G.
  • Offline patient IDs, billing engine (ICD-10 → CPT/HCPCS), and phone-to-phone sync over Bluetooth / local WiFi when offline.

Country configurations exist for South Africa, Ethiopia, and Malawi today, with regulatory data drafted for Kenya, Nigeria, the US, UK, and India.

5. Multimodal capture: 8 clinical artifacts, one button

Clinicians work with paper. ChartLite has one "📷 Capture clinical artifact" button that uses Gemma 4 e4b's vision to read any of eight artifact types and pipe the result into the BODHI safety check:

Artifact What gets extracted
Pill bottle / medication package Drug, dose, route, frequency, expiry, manufacturer
Lab report Test name, value, unit, reference range, flag
Rapid diagnostic test (malaria / HIV / COVID) Test type, result, visible bands
Vital-signs device (BP cuff, pulse oximeter) Vital name, value, unit
Referral letter From facility, diagnosis, reason, urgency
Vaccine card / Yellow Card Vaccine, date, dose number, batch, route
Handwritten prescription Drug, dose, route, frequency, duration
Discharge summary Discharge diagnosis, meds, follow-up, alerts

Tap → photo → Gemma 4 reads the image and produces a structured record → Gemma 4 then chooses for itself which safety checks to run (drug-drug interaction? drug-allergy clash? wrong drug for this condition? emergency triage?) → results render as a card with the structured fields, the tool calls the model chose, and any alerts.

The judge sees Gemma 4 actively choosing which tools to call. That is how a 4-billion-parameter model becomes safe in a clinical setting: multimodal perception, structured tool calls, deterministic medical lookups.

6. Real-world deployment readiness

Privacy. Voice and language model run on the phone, so the patient's voice and record never leave the device unless the clinician syncs. SMS relay encrypts each visit with the algorithm online banking uses (AES-256-GCM), packing a whole encounter into one 160-character SMS.

Footprint. Gemma 4 e2b in 4-bit-quantized form (a quarter the original size, minimal quality loss) is ~1.6 GB on disk, runs on 2 GB-RAM phones. Gemma 4 e4b is ~3.1 GB, runs on 4 GB-RAM mid-range phones. Our dev hardware is a Galaxy Fold 7 (12 GB RAM, Gemma 4 e4b); reading a clinical photo and producing the structured record plus the safety-tool trace takes 30–50 seconds end-to-end — the model picks which BODHI checks to run, then runs them. The captured wall-clock in our demo video is honest, no speed-ramps.

Governance. BODHI is licensed by Eka Care under CC BY-NC 4.0 — free for non-commercial use, commercial deployment requires an Eka license. Benchmark and methodology are independent (Apache 2.0). ChartLite has not been deployed in a clinic — it is production-ready and awaiting pilot.

7. What we built for this hackathon

In eight days:

  • Gemma 4 vision: wired into the existing model dispatcher so one language-model session handles both text and image inputs.
  • Three new artifact prompts (vaccine card, handwritten prescription, discharge summary) on top of five we already had.
  • Function-calling refactor: four safety checks (drug-drug, drug-allergy, drug-for-condition, triage urgency) rewired so Gemma 4 chooses which to invoke per artifact.
  • Universal capture button on the encounter screen.
  • Multimodal benchmark: ~105 images across 8 artifact types from WHO templates and public sources. Per-type accuracy and per-field precision/recall on a new dashboard tab.
  • Default model flip: recommended on-device model is now Gemma 4 e4b (was Qwen 3.5), based on the benchmark evidence above.

8. How to verify everything

  • Benchmarks: git clone .../clinical-edge-bench, pip install -r scripts/requirements.txt, python3 scripts/benchmark_pharmacology_mcqa.py --models gemma4-e4b. Raw per-(model, case) JSON preserved for every run.
  • App: git clone .../chartlite, ./gradlew assembleDebug produces an APK for Android 8+. First launch downloads Gemma 4 e4b from HuggingFace.
  • Dashboard: benchmark.chartlite.health — every number links to its raw JSON. The Methodology and Model-Versions tabs openly declare the synthetic-data and self-bias soft spots and record the exact API model IDs + Ollama tags used.

If a judge wants to disprove a single number, they have the raw data. That's the bar we want for clinical AI generally.
